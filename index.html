Your starting point

What should you know before choosing a TEFL course?

01. Understand TEFL

TEFL stands for Teaching English as a Foreign Language. Courses introduce methods for planning lessons, teaching language skills, and supporting English learners.

02. Check the requirements

Teaching eligibility varies by employer and destination. Research degree, visa, experience, and certification requirements before paying for training.

03. Compare course value

Look beyond the sticker price. Review training hours, curriculum, provider credentials, tutor support, and career resources before deciding.

Is teaching abroad right for you?

Whether you're exploring a career change, considering an international move, or interested in language education, start by answering a few practical questions.

  • Where would you like to teach, and what qualifications do employers there require?
  • Do you want online training you can complete around your schedule?
  • What is your total budget for certification, travel, and relocation?
  • What type of teaching role and learner age group interests you?

TEFL certification is training—not a guarantee of employment, visa eligibility, or placement.

Common questions

Do I need a TEFL certificate to teach abroad?

Requirements differ by destination and employer. Some roles ask for TEFL training, while others have additional degree, visa, or experience requirements.

Is a 120-hour course enough?

120 hours is a commonly referenced training length, but the appropriate credential depends on the job and destination. Check the specific employer's requirements.
