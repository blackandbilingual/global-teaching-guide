How to Get Started Teaching English Abroad

Curious about teaching English in another country—or online? Here's a practical place to start, from understanding TEFL certification to comparing training options.

First, what is TEFL?

TEFL stands for Teaching English as a Foreign Language. TEFL training typically introduces teaching techniques such as lesson planning, classroom management, explaining language concepts, and helping learners practice English skills.

A TEFL certificate may be useful for some teaching roles, but it is not a universal qualification. Employers and countries can have different expectations, including education, experience, work authorization, or visa requirements.

Before signing up for a course, research these four things

1. Your destination or teaching format

Teaching in a classroom abroad and teaching English online can involve different requirements. Start with the type of work you actually want, then look at real job listings and official visa guidance for your destination.

2. Employer qualifications

Check whether roles require a bachelor's degree, previous teaching experience, a particular certification, or other credentials. Do this before paying for training.

3. Your budget and timeline

Certification is only one possible expense. If you plan to relocate, think about travel, housing deposits, documents, and the time it may take to qualify and apply.

4. The kind of training you need

Consider whether you learn best with flexible online lessons, instructor feedback, practical teaching exercises, or more structured support.

Worth remembering: Completing a TEFL course does not guarantee a job, a visa, or admission to a particular teaching program.

How to evaluate a TEFL certification course

One common starting point is a 120-hour TEFL course. But the number of hours is only one factor. Read the syllabus, investigate the training provider, and compare what support is included.

When comparing programs, look for clear information about course content, instructor support, assessment methods, provider credentials, career resources, and the full price.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need TEFL certification to teach English abroad?

It depends on the employer, country, and role. Review job requirements and relevant official guidance for your destination.

Is a 120-hour TEFL course enough?

Some employers reference 120 hours, but that does not mean the same course qualifies you for every role. Check individual requirements.

Can I take TEFL training online?

Yes, online training options exist. Compare course structure, assessments, and whether the credential fits your intended teaching path.
